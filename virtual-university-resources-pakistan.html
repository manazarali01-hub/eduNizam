A repeatable study workflow for VU courses

Use the same process for a mathematics, statistics, computer science or general education course. The course code helps you find the right material, but the current course outline and VULMS announcements determine what you should prepare for your enrolled semester.

1. Make a course checklist

Write the course code and title, then list the topics or lectures covered in your current semester. Use the current course outline and instructor announcements; do not assume an older paper follows the same coverage.

2. Learn before memorising

Study one small topic from the official handout or lecture. Close the material and write its key idea in your own words. For a formula, also note what each symbol means, the conditions for using it and one example.

3. Practise without notes

Attempt a short quiz or a few questions with the notes closed. For a numerical question, show each step and check arithmetic. For a conceptual question, explain why the answer is correct rather than relying only on recognition.

4. Review the exact mistake

Record the topic, your incorrect step and the correction. Separate a forgotten definition from a misunderstood concept or calculation error. Revisit that specific gap, then solve a different question to see whether the correction holds.

5. Use past papers carefully

Attempt a past paper under timed conditions after revising the relevant topics. Treat recall questions and community solutions as practice only: they may be incomplete, inaccurate or from a different course version. Confirm examinable material through current VULMS instructions.

6. Plan the next session

At the end, mark each topic as not started, learning, or able to solve independently. Begin the next session with one weak topic and a short retrieval quiz. A completed checklist is more useful when it reflects what you can answer without help.

Where each kind of material fits

  • Course outline and VULMS: current semester instructions, deadlines, quizzes, assignments and official announcements.
  • OpenCourseWare and handouts: structured explanations and lecture material where the relevant course is available.
  • Past papers and community notes: extra practice, question-style familiarity and revision—not a replacement for the current course outline.
  • Answer keys: a way to check your attempt after solving, not a substitute for doing the question yourself.
Source rule: Community notes and past papers are supplementary. Current handouts, syllabus, quizzes and announcements should be confirmed through official VU sources.
