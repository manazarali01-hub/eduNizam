A reliable workflow for textbooks and research

Use this checklist to avoid studying from an outdated syllabus, downloading the wrong edition or relying on a journal claim that has not been checked.

1. Match class, subject and board

Before downloading a textbook, confirm the grade, subject, medium, academic year and relevant provincial or national curriculum. Similar file names may refer to different editions or syllabus versions.

2. Confirm the current syllabus

Look for the latest official notification, scheme of studies or syllabus update from the responsible authority. If a reduced or smart syllabus is announced, verify its year and examination session before using it.

3. Build a usable study set

Keep the prescribed textbook, syllabus and teacher's course plan together. Mark chapters and learning outcomes, then use videos or simulations to clarify difficult ideas rather than as a replacement for required reading.

4. Check research access and journal status

For scholarly work, check access conditions through your institution and use the relevant HEC service to verify journal recognition or category. A journal website's own claim is not independent confirmation.

5. Record sources and dates

Save the official page or document title, publisher, publication/update date and access date. For research, record complete citation details and distinguish peer-reviewed evidence from commentary or promotional material.

6. Recheck before an exam or submission

Curriculum notices, access permissions and journal classifications can change. Revisit the official source before relying on a document for an upcoming examination, thesis or grant application.

Good practice: prefer official downloadable material, respect the publisher's terms, and never upload confidential student records or unpublished research data to an untrusted service.
